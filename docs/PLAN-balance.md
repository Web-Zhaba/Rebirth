# Rebirth — balance plan (growth past 1000 residents)

Goal: the early pace from `PLAN-pace.md` stays, growth slows down past ~1000 residents because
the town needs more organisation (logistics, goods chains, administration), not because of a hard cap.
Measured pace (player, 2026-10-26): 100 residents in ~1.5 h real time at 4× (~75 game days).

## Milestones

| # | Milestone | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Builder limits | A building site takes at most a crew sized by its footprint (`siteCrew`: about one hand per 12 m², 2..16); a толока doubles it. A road edge takes about one hand per 10 m of its length (1..`ROAD_CREW_MAX` = 12). Hands no site can use stay idle instead of piling onto the same sites; the idle artel is visible to the player. **Done**: `siteCrew`, `FOCUS_CREWS`, `roadCrew` (`sim/buildings.ts`, `sim/roads.ts`), builders fill crews in `sim/life.ts`, the builders panel shows idle hands and a per-site estimate. Pace harness: early milestones unchanged (10 houses 1:15), population on game day 128 is 149 instead of 244. |
| B2 | Families by house level | A house holds as many families as its level. A new house starts at level 1 (the large house at 2); the area of its lot (old foundations scale it) sets the highest level (`maxLevel` = families the area holds + 1). Upgrades go level by level, each costing more (`UPGRADE` × current level) under the same service, land and mood conditions; capacity = level × family size. Old saves: a house keeps every family living in it (its level is raised to their number). **Done**: `startLevel`, `maxLevel`, `upgradable`, `upgradeLabor`, `upgradeMaterials` in `sim/buildings.ts`; houses on old foundations cost their template; save migration in `sim/save.ts`. |
| B3 | Goods groups | `wares` splits into clothing, dishes, tools and furnishings (`sim/goods.ts`). Clothing comes from a new weaving workshop (craft zone) and the evening home crafts, dishes from the pottery, furnishings from the carpentry (planks), tools from the smithy (logs). Households need clothing (tents and level 1), plus dishes at level 2, plus furnishings at level 3+; the shops and markets hand them out, the share served feeds mood as before and is kept per household. A house is raised to the next level only while every family in it got at least 80 % of its goods at the last midnight (no level loss for now). Tools wear out with work: woodcutters, sawmills, quarries, farms and the goods workshops use them and work at half rate without. Calls for settlers pay in clothing and dishes; every good is traded on its own; the top bar keeps one goods chip with a line per good. Old saves split their wares by a fixed share. **Done**: `sim/goods.ts`, weaving workshop in the craft zone, `Household.served`, `houseGoods` in the snapshot and the upgrade check, the tools notice once a game day, old trade orders in wares trade clothing. Pace harness: 10 houses 1:15, population 100 at 5:04, game day 138 with 126 residents (was 149 after B1–B2). |
| B4 | Logistics and storages | Resources live in storages, not one town stock: a log yard (logs, planks), a stone yard, the barn and cellar (food), a warehouse (goods). Producers deliver to the nearest storage with room, sites and households fetch from the nearest one that holds it; carrying takes carters' time by road distance, so a sprawling town without district storages slows down. Carts are visible on roads. **Done**: `sim/storage.ts` (`Ledger`: `SimState.stores` per store, `stock` stays the total; `put`/`take` by road distance, `haulHours`, `sync` for flows with no place of their own), store rooms in `STORES`/`CAMP_STORE` (`sim/buildings.ts`; the camp holds a little of everything), the timber yard, stone yard and warehouse as kinds of the storehouse tool (a switch, no new buttons), producers lose the carting share of their hours (`prod.haul`) and stop with a notice when no store has room (their hands join the artel), sites add the carting to their labor (`Building.haul`), households eat, dress and heat from the stores nearest to their home, spoilage per store, carts drive to the store they fill; old saves spread their stock over their stores. Pace harness (builds a yard of a kind when its room runs low): 10 houses 1:18, population 100 at 5:23 (day 70); era II on day 128 with 194 residents (B3: day 138, 126) — full yards stop the woodcutter and its hands go to the farms, so the harvests finish; the producers of the harness stand by the camp, so the carting costs little there. |
| B5 | Finite resources | Forest stands are felled and regrow over years; woodcutters work the forest within their radius and move or stop when it is cut. Quarries and fishing grounds deplete slowly and recover partly. Overlays show what is left. |
| B6 | Immigration by attractiveness | Settlers come by the town's attractiveness (food outlook, mood, services coverage, free homes, jobs), not once per vacant house; the inflow per day saturates with size so a big town grows by births and steady arrivals. The town panel explains the score. |
| B7 | Administration | A town hall and later district offices give a management capacity of N residents each; past it every work rate drops gently (a visible «перегрузка управления»). New buildings for B3–B7 come as variants or upgrades of existing tools where possible (see the memory note on the build bar). |
| B8 | Museums | New potential museum sites on the map (old buildings that can become museums for found items): design to be agreed with the user before work starts. |
| B9 | Harness and tuning | `npm run pace` plays to 2000 residents, reports what limits growth at each stage (time per `growthAdvice` reason, idle hands, goods shortage days) and is calibrated against the measured pace; constants tuned to a target curve (fast to ~100, slowing past ~1000). |
| Later | Fires and disease | Density risks: fires spread between close wooden houses, disease in crowded quarters without wells and healers. |

Order: B1 → B2 → B3 → B4 → B6 → B5 → B7 → B9 → B8 (needs a design talk).
